::: keypoints
- Engagement can happen before a decision, while it’s being made, and after it takes effect.
- Groups outside government work at every stage, from early meetings with officials to appeals and ballot campaigns.
- Having a chance to engage isn’t the same as being able to use it. Schedules, language, distance, internet access, and trust all get in the way.
- For every stage, ask: who is missing, and who keeps getting asked?
:::

Engagement can happen before a decision is made, while it is being made, and after it takes effect. Each stage offers different ways in, and groups outside government work at every stage too.

The three stages of public engagement. Each one has its own page in this section.
:::

::: wide
| Stage | What happens | Common examples | How outside groups try to influence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Before** | Notice, education, due diligence | Public notices, mailing lists, staff reports, explainers | Meeting with officials early, shaping which options are considered |
| **During** | Feedback, deliberation | Public comment, hearings, advisory committees, civic assemblies | Organized testimony, letters, sign-on campaigns |
| **After** | Accountability, appeal | Reports on results, audits, appeals, ballot measures | Legal appeals, lawsuits, ballot campaigns |
:::

**Who gets to take part?** Having a chance to engage is not the same as being able to use it. Meetings may happen during working hours, go on for hours, or follow rules that limit what people can say. {src: Utah book} Barriers in Central Oregon include language and interpretation, disability access, work and caregiving schedules, distance and transportation, internet access, and trust in institutions. These look different across ages: a working parent, a retiree, and a high school student each face different hurdles. Two questions to keep in mind for every stage: who is missing, and who keeps getting asked? {src: Taha Elwefati, CC Mtg 1, Sep 25; Andrea Wasilew, CC Mtg 3, Oct 9; Kathi Barguil and Rachel Haakenson, CC Mtg 4 Pt 1, Oct 14; Karen Rush, Oct 15; USDOT, Promising Practices (2022)}
