::: keypoints
- Engagement can happen before a decision, while it’s being made, and after it takes effect.
- Before: learn about the issue and its impacts. During: give feedback so proposals improve. After: check that the policy works as intended.
- At every stage, residents, companies, and others outside government try to shape decisions to meet their interests.
:::

Engagement can happen before the government makes a decision, while it is being made, and after it takes effect. At each stage, there are important opportunities for residents, corporations, and others outside the government to shape the decisions to meet their interests.

::: figure
{{> phases}}

The three stages of public engagement. Each one has its own page in this section.
:::

::: wide
| Stage | Why | Common examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Before** (notice, education) | Opportunities to learn about the issue and its impacts, so that later engagement is well-informed and impactful | Public notices, mailing lists, staff reports, explainers, educational sessions |
| **During** (feedback, deliberation) | Offering feedback on what the proposed decisions do well and poorly, so that they can be improved before they are implemented | Public comment, hearings, advisory committees, civic assemblies |
| **After** (accountability, appeals) | Checking in to make sure that the implemented policy is doing what it was intended to do, with reasonable resources | Regular reporting on results, audits, appeals, oversight bodies, lawsuits |
:::
