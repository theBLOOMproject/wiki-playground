::: keypoints
- In Oregon, the public has a right to watch most government decisions being made, but usually not a right to speak, except in cases like land use and budget hearings.
- The law guarantees openings in public meetings, land use, budgets, state rulemaking, utilities, and tax incentive decisions.
- How much residents can change a land use project depends on timing: the most when the rules are set, very little once a project is allowed “by right.”
- Executive offices and staff can make many decisions with little public review.
:::

**The “floor” is what the law requires. In Oregon, the public has a right to watch most government decisions being made, but usually not a right to speak, except in certain cases like land use and budget hearings.** Governing bodies set their own rules for public comment. {added: [City of Springfield summary of OGEC training](https://springfield-or.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Memo-Oregon-Public-Meeting-Law_Final.pdf); [Deschutes County guide to Public Meetings Law](https://www.deschutescounty.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/administration/page/26674/reference_guide_-_public_meetings_law.pdf)}

## Where the public must be allowed in

| Opportunity | What the law requires |
| --- | --- |
| **Public meetings** ([Oregon Public Meetings Law](https://www.oregon.gov/transparency/pages/public-meetings.aspx)) | A quorum must meet in public, with advance notice and minutes. When a body takes in-person testimony, it must also allow remote testimony. |
| **Land use** | Planning commissions and hearings officers hear land use cases, with mailed notice to nearby owners (see [Notice](#before-a-decision)). Oregon’s Statewide Planning Goal 1 requires citizen involvement in local planning. {added: Goal 1; add link} |
| **Budgets** | Oregon budget law requires every city, county, and district to have a citizen budget committee, and the public can comment at budget hearings. {src: Who Governs Central Oregon} |
| **State rules** | Before a state agency adopts a rule, it must publish notice at least 21 days ahead and accept comments. If 10 people (or a group with 10 members) ask, it must hold a hearing. Anyone can sign up for an agency’s rule notices. {added: [ORS 183.335](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors183.html)} |
| **State boards and commissions** | The Governor appoints members to more than 265 state boards and commissions, with openings posted on oregon.gov. {src: Who Governs Central Oregon} |
| **Utilities and water** | Residents can comment in Oregon Public Utility Commission cases and on water right applications with the Oregon Water Resources Department, and vote in electric cooperative board elections. The 2025 POWER Act created a separate rate class so large data centers pay their own grid costs, but it covers only investor-owned utilities (Pacific Power, PGE, Idaho Power). Co-ops like Central Electric Cooperative answer to member-elected boards instead. {src: Who Governs Central Oregon; EDCO written submission, Oct 13} {verify: against PUC Data Center Advisory Committee summary, Jun 26, 2026} |
| **Business tax incentives** | Enterprise zone agreements longer than three years need city and county resolutions adopted at public meetings. The company must file before breaking ground, and denials can be appealed to the Oregon Tax Court. The Strategic Investment Program is a 15-year tax break with an annual community service fee paid to local taxing districts. {src: CC Mtg 4 Pt 1, Oct 14, Kelsey Haskett in chat} |

**Don’t forget the simplest options.** Anyone can call or email an elected official, or talk to one at the grocery store. People can also work through organizations and advocacy groups that follow an issue.

## Limits on the floor

**Land use timing.** How much residents can affect a project depends on when they get involved. {src: Who Governs Central Oregon; Research Doc} {verify: legal specifics}

| Stage | What is happening | What residents can still change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Setting the rules** | A city or county adopts or changes its comprehensive plan or zoning | The most: what kinds of projects are allowed, and where |
| **Discretionary permit** | A project needs a hearing to show it meets set criteria | Whether it meets those criteria, and what conditions are attached |
| **Allowed outright** | The project is already allowed under existing zoning (“by right”) | Very little; staff may approve it without a hearing {gap: local example} |

**Legislative versus executive decisions.** Councils, commissions, and the Legislature set policy in public meetings. Executive offices and staff carry out that policy, and can make decisions or set priorities with little public review. In Prineville, community input on enterprise zone agreements came through elected officials (the mayor, city council, and county commissioners), with no separate public process, though the City said the climate could be different today. {src: CC Mtg 2, Oct 1}

The takeaway: the landscape is complex. Which rules apply depends on who is deciding, what kind of decision it is, and how far along it is.
