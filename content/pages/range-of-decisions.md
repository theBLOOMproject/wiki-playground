::: keypoints
- Research found about 29 AI-related decisions by public bodies affecting the three counties between late 2023 and October 2026, from data centers to policing and schools.
- From government’s perspective, these boil down to three types: government use, permissions and planning, and regulations.
- Public input on government purchases is uncommon, though oversight bodies and contract renewals offer other chances.
- Planning decides whether, where, and on what terms projects like data centers can locate. Regulation reins in possible harms.
:::

One of the main places where Central Oregonians can have a say in how AI shows up in their lives is through **state and local government decisions.** Our research found about 29 AI-related decisions made by public bodies affecting Deschutes, Crook, and Jefferson counties between late 2023 and October 2026. These decisions ranged from data centers, policing, schools, government operations, and more ([Central Oregon Decisions tracker](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/19zmUMXSqIPJhdJZTfiAFqM_DHhTYhB1pbOovrmJVmks/edit?gid=1770817539#gid=1770817539)).

**From the government’s perspective, these boil down to three types of decisions:**

::: wide
| Type | What government is deciding | Key opportunities | Where the public usually fits in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **[Government use](#government-use)** | Whether and how government itself uses AI tools | Requests for qualifications, contracts, AI use policies | Discussions around use policies may happen, but engagement on procurement decisions is rare |
| **[Permissions and planning](#planning-permissions)** | Whether and where private AI investments (like data centers) can locate, and on what terms | Economic plans, comprehensive plans, growth boundaries, permits, tax agreements | Hearings on long-range plans and specific projects |
| **[Regulations](#regulations)** | How to rein in possible harms from private industry | Laws, ordinances, administrative rules, utility orders | Agenda setting, advisory bodies, public hearings |
:::

The following pages describe each of these types of decisions in more detail.

::: cards
- [Government use](#government-use) — How governments buy AI tools and set rules for their own use of AI, and where the public fits in.
- [Planning and permissions](#planning-permissions) — How communities decide whether, where, and on what terms projects like data centers can locate.
- [Regulations](#regulations) — How laws, ordinances, and utility rules rein in possible harms, and how residents help shape them.
:::
