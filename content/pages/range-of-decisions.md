::: keypoints
- AI already shows up in local businesses, data centers, classrooms, policing, and government offices.
- This Assembly focuses on decisions government is making about AI, and how the public can influence them.
- These decisions come in three types: government use of AI, permissive planning, and restrictive regulations.
:::

AI is already part of daily life in Central Oregon, sometimes in ways that we know about, and sometimes in ways that are harder to see. It shows up in local businesses, in the region’s growing number of data centers, in classrooms, in policing, and inside government offices.

What we are most focused on during this Assembly are the **decisions that the government is making related to AI, and how the public can influence those decisions.** For example, an issue like “how to manage AI in the classroom” involves school boards across the region creating new technology use policies, with some guidance from the state Department of Education. We found many examples of these decisions at various levels of government, including:

- The City of Bend developing guidance for how city workers use AI, and figuring out which uses of AI are desired and beneficial for the public
- Multiple school boards procuring new AI to support educational outcomes in the classroom
- The City of Prineville discussing new data centers in its economic planning strategies, and how additional revenue from these projects should be used
- Organizers in Bend turning out to shut down the use of AI-powered surveillance systems in their city
- And many more

**We have broken these down into three types of decisions: what AI is used for, planning decisions, and restrictive regulations.** As you think about the AI-related issues that matter most to you, try to identify where they fit in this map.

::: wide
| Type | What government is deciding | Key opportunities | Where the public usually fits in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **[Uses of AI technology](#government-use)** | Whether and how government itself uses AI tools (for classroom support, public benefits, application review, etc.) | Requests for qualifications, contracts, AI use policies | Discussions around use policies may happen, but engagement on procurement decisions is rare |
| **[Permissive planning](#planning-permissions)** | Whether and where private AI investments (like data centers) can locate, and on what terms | Economic plans, comprehensive plans, growth boundaries, permits, tax agreements | Hearings on long-range plans and specific projects |
| **[Restrictive regulations](#regulations)** | How to rein in possible harms from private industry | Laws, ordinances, administrative rules, utility orders | Agenda setting, advisory bodies, public hearings |
:::
