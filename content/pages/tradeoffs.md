---
title: "What tradeoffs need to be considered to create wise public policy?"
status: draft
---

::: keypoints
- Creating policy means weighing goals that pull against each other.
- Four tradeoffs to think about: cost and attention, expertise, point in the process, and level of influence.
:::

Creating policy means weighing goals that pull against each other. Here are a few questions to stimulate your thinking.

- **Cost and attention.** Demanding thorough, deliberative public engagement on every single issue would quickly sap the attention that public administrators have to do their jobs and cost a lot of money. Where should the extra effort be applied, or how do you strike that balance?
- **Expertise.** Many decisions related to AI require specialized knowledge to make an informed decision, whether related to the technology itself, administrative workings, economics, or otherwise. What are you okay with leaving to experts, and where do you want the voices of everyday people to take precedence? How can these things work well together?
- **Point in the process.** At which points would you like to see more public engagement in local decisions regarding AI? When in a project’s life could public input considerably change the decisions that are made?
- **Level of Influence.** Some would argue that we elect our representatives for a reason, and we should leave them to do their jobs. And giving residents direct control / influence over any public matters comes with its own risks. What kind of decisions should the public be able to participate in, and how much decision-making power should they have (from purely advisory to making it law)?
