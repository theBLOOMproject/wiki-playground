*Note: This section describes how engagement works today, and broadly what it is meant to achieve. It does not define what “good” engagement means; **that is left to delegates.***

About 266,000 people live in Crook, Deschutes, and Jefferson counties, and nearly every part of their daily lives is shaped by state and local government. (Census, July 2025 estimates: [Deschutes](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/deschutescountyoregon), [Crook](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/crookcountyoregon), [Jefferson](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/jeffersoncountyoregon).)

- **Land and natural resources:** Most land in the region is public, almost all of it federal: more than 77% of Deschutes County and more than half of Crook County ([USDA NRCS, Deschutes](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/state-offices/oregon/deschutes-county); [Crook](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/state-offices/oregon/crook-county)). It is managed by the [Deschutes National Forest](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/deschutes), [Ochoco National Forest](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/ochoco), and [BLM Prineville District](https://www.blm.gov/office/prineville-district-office). The [Oregon Department of Forestry](https://www.oregon.gov/odf/aboutodf/pages/mapoffices.aspx) runs its Central Oregon District from Prineville.
- **Zoning and building:** Counties handle zoning and permits outside city limits (for example, [Deschutes County Community Development](https://www.deschutescounty.gov/640/Zoning-101) and [Crook County Planning](https://crookcountyor.gov/1307/Planning-Department)). Cities handle them inside city limits.
- **Traffic and roads:** [ODOT Region 4](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/regions/pages/index.aspx) runs state highways. County road departments run county roads ([Deschutes](https://www.deschutescounty.gov/259/Road), [Crook](https://crookcountyor.gov/1250/Road-Department)). Each city runs its own streets.
- **Schools:** Each school district has its own elected board: Bend-La Pine, Redmond, Sisters, Crook County, Jefferson County, Culver, Black Butte, and Ashwood. The [Oregon Department of Education](https://www.oregon.gov/ode/about-us/Documents/CombinedDirectory_20260922_034305.pdf) sets statewide standards.
- **Public health:** Each county runs a health department ([Deschutes](https://www.deschutescounty.gov/265/Health-Services), [Crook](https://crookcountyor.gov/1260/Health-Department), [Jefferson](https://www.jeffersoncountyor.gov/publichealth/page/community-services)), overseen by the [Oregon Health Authority](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ph/providerpartnerresources/localhealthdepartmentresources/pages/lhd.aspx).
- **Economy:** Cities and counties handle planning, permits, and incentives like enterprise zones. The state works in the region through the Governor’s [Central Oregon Regional Solutions team](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/regional-solutions/Pages/Central.aspx). The nonprofit [Economic Development for Central Oregon (EDCO)](https://www.edcoinfo.com/about-edco) recruits and supports businesses.

Decisions about artificial intelligence are no exception. They land in many of these same places: school boards set AI use policies, cities and counties approve data center sites and tax incentives, and state agencies set rules for their own use of AI. *(See [How are AI-related decisions made?](#range-of-decisions))*

People who live in Oregon have the right and the opportunity to provide input into the decisions that affect them. We affect some of those decisions through electing representatives, but we also have other means to weigh in on specific decisions.

**Effective public engagement helps ensure that public sector decisions are informed not only by technical expertise, but also by the knowledge, values, priorities, and personal experiences of the people who will be affected by those decisions and whose tax dollars support them.** {src: Utah book}

Examples of formal public engagement in state and local decisions include:

- **State legislative hearings,** and city council and county commission meetings that are open to the public and invite public comment
- **Town halls** on specific topics
- **Public comment periods** when local or state rules are being drafted
- **Public meetings** organized by local or state elected officials
- **Initiatives and referenda,** through which citizens and advocacy groups can place items on the ballot for the public to approve or deny
- **Boards and commissions,** where citizens may be appointed
- And, of course, **elections**
