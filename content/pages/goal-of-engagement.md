*Note: This section describes how engagement works today, and broadly what it is meant to achieve. It does not define what “good” engagement means; **that is left to delegates.***

About 266,000 people live in Crook, Deschutes, and Jefferson counties, and nearly every part of their daily lives is shaped by state and local government. (Census, July 2025 estimates: [Deschutes](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/deschutescountyoregon), [Crook](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/crookcountyoregon), [Jefferson](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/jeffersoncountyoregon).) This includes decisions about how our land and natural resources are managed, what kind of businesses are allowed to be here and where (zoning), how our schools are managed, how public health is protected, and much more. Each of these is managed by one or more departments at the city, county, and state levels (or sometimes federal, in the case of certain public lands).

People who live in Oregon have the right and the opportunity to provide input into the decisions that affect them. We affect some of those decisions through electing representatives, but we also have other means to weigh in on specific decisions.

**Effective public engagement helps ensure that public sector decisions are informed not only by technical expertise, but also by the knowledge, values, priorities, and personal experiences of the people who will be affected by those decisions and whose tax dollars support them.**

Examples of formal public engagement in state and local decisions include:

- **State legislative hearings,** and city council and county commission meetings that are open to the public and invite public comment
- **Town halls** on specific topics
- **Surveys**
- **Public comment periods** when local or state rules are being drafted
- **Public meetings** organized by local or state elected officials
- **Public hearings** required by state and local law for local legislation and quasi-judicial land use applications (for example, code updates, conditional use permits, and zone changes)
- **Initiatives and referenda,** through which citizens and advocacy groups can place items on the ballot for the public to approve or deny
- **Boards and commissions,** where citizens may be appointed
- And, of course, **elections**
