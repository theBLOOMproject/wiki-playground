The decision
: Whether Bend police should use AI-powered stationary license plate reader cameras, and from which company

Who had authority
: Bend Police Department and City Manager (small purchases); City Council (larger contracts, or when it chooses to weigh in)

The public’s role
: Little to none before the cameras went up. Afterward: a petition, public comment at council meetings, and advocacy by local groups

Competing views
: Police say the cameras help investigate crimes like organized retail theft. Residents and advocates worry about privacy and about federal immigration officials accessing the data

What happened afterward
: Cameras turned off in January 2026. The city is now considering a different vendor, with a promised council vote and public input

- **Before:** The Bend Police Department used grant money to buy four Flock cameras, placed at two Highway 97 intersections. Because the purchase was small, it did not need a council vote ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/01/08/bend-flock-cameras-ai-license-plate-camera-law-enforcement/)).
- **During:** In June 2025, because a national search setting was left on, federal immigration officials searched Bend’s camera data 279 times ([The Source](https://www.bendsource.com/news/localnews/bend-officials-commit-to-council-vote-public-discussion-regarding-stationary-surveillance-camera-contract-add-on/)). Residents signed a Change.org petition and packed council meetings in early January 2026. The Latino Community Association presented to the council.
- **After:** On Jan. 7, 2026, the city manager decided not to renew the Flock contract, and the council directed staff to turn the cameras off. In March 2026, the state passed Senate Bill 1516, setting new rules for license plate readers. When the city later moved to buy stationary cameras from a different company (Axon), a local privacy advocate raised concerns at a council meeting. The mayor then committed to a council vote with public input ([The Source](https://www.bendsource.com/news/localnews/bend-officials-commit-to-council-vote-public-discussion-regarding-stationary-surveillance-camera-contract-add-on/)).

**Lesson:** For small purchases, the public often learns about AI tools only after they are in use. Public pressure afterward can still change course and can push officials to open future decisions to public input.
