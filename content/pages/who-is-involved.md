title: "Who gets involved in public decisions?"
status: draft
---

::: keypoints
- Government makes the final decision, but residents, organized groups, advocacy groups, businesses, and public-private coordinators all take part.
- Residents who organize tend to have more influence, because officials see them as speaking for a broader group.
- Decisions involving companies, like software contracts and data center deals, are often less visible to the public.
- EDCO says early recruitment is kept confidential for good reasons, and that public input comes through elected officials, its strategy meetings, and public votes on long-term incentives.
:::

Our state and local governments may be responsible for making the final decisions, but many groups take part in public decisions. Each plays a different role.

::: wide
| Who | What they do | Central Oregon examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Government** | Makes and carries out decisions. State decisions often set the rules that local governments must follow. | State agencies, county commissions, city councils, school boards, special districts, public advisory commissions made up of members of the public |
| **Individuals** | Every resident has the right to take part in local decisions, both through formal means (public meetings, comment periods, public hearings, etc.) and informal means (organizing petitions, direct actions, etc.). | Speaking at a council meeting, emailing a commissioner, etc. |
| **Organized residents** | When residents organize, officials tend to see them as speaking for a broader group, not just one person. | |
| **Advocacy groups** | Speak for a cause or community. They often follow issues closely and comment on behalf of members, or file lawsuits. | Central Oregon LandWatch, The Environmental Center, 1000 Friends of Oregon, Oregon Rural Action, Bend Privacy Alliance, etc. |
| **Businesses and business groups** | Often have significant sway. They may sell services to government, seek incentives to locate here, or hire lobbyists to advocate on their behalf. | Local businesses, AI companies, real estate developers or agents |
| **Public-private coordinators** | Separate organizations that develop strategies for the development of the region and coordinate across the public and private spheres. Organizations like EDCO may also be involved in recruiting, keeping, and supporting businesses that move to the region. | Economic Development for Central Oregon, Central Oregon Intergovernmental Council |
:::

## Public-private negotiations

Many government decisions related to AI in particular involve companies that are selling services to the government (for example, MagicSchool AI selling its software to local school districts), or working with the government to develop their operations in the area (for example, data center developments). Compared to decisions that go through local councils or planning commissions, these are often less visible to the public.

**On the other hand, there could also be good reasons for these private dealings to remain private.** Economic Development for Central Oregon (EDCO) says early recruitment is often kept confidential to protect companies’ competitive information and workers. It calls these confidentiality agreements a real limit, not bad faith, though it often means the public learns of a project after major terms are set. EDCO believes the appropriate places for public input are elected officials, its three-year community strategy meetings, and public votes on long-term incentives.
