::: keypoints
- Government makes the final decision, but residents, organized groups, advocacy groups, businesses, and public-private coordinators all try to shape it.
- Residents who organize tend to have more influence, because officials see them as speaking for a broader group.
- Decisions involving companies are often less transparent: early recruitment is often confidential, and companies may hire lobbyists.
- Lobbyists who work with the Legislature or state officials must register and file reports anyone can search.
:::

Our state and local governments may be responsible for making the final decisions, but many groups take part in public decisions. Each plays a different role.

::: wide
| Who | What they do | Central Oregon examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Government** | Makes and carries out decisions. State decisions often set the rules that local governments must follow. | State agencies, county commissions, city councils, school boards, special districts |
| **Individuals** | Every resident has the right to take part in local decisions, both through formal means (public meetings, comment periods, etc.) and informal means (organizing petitions, direct actions, etc.). | Speaking at a council meeting, emailing a commissioner |
| **Organized residents** | When residents organize, officials tend to see them as speaking for a broader group, not just one person. | La Pine residents’ response to a proposed data center |
| **Advocacy groups** | Speak for a cause or community. They often follow issues closely and comment on behalf of members, or file lawsuits when necessary. | Central Oregon LandWatch, Bend Privacy Alliance |
| **Businesses and business groups** | Often have significant sway. They may sell services to government, seek incentives to locate here, or hire lobbyists. | Local businesses, AI companies, real estate |
| **Public-private coordinators** | Separate organizations that develop strategies for the development of the region and coordinate across the public and private spheres. Organizations like EDCO may also be involved in recruiting, keeping, and supporting businesses that move to the region. | Economic Development for Central Oregon, Central Oregon Intergovernmental Council |
:::

**What works?** Rachel Haakenson (NeighborImpact) listed what tends to work: legal process and past precedent, fit with the organization’s stated values, what partners and funders think, a formal letter to the executive director, and many voices rather than one survey response. {src: CC Mtg 3, Oct 9}

**Also:** Many government decisions related to AI involve companies that are selling services to the government, or working with the government to locate their services in the area. Compared to legislative decisions, these are often less transparent for a variety of reasons:

- Companies may also hire people to meet with officials, build relationships, and influence decisions (lobbying). In Oregon, people who lobby the Legislature or state officials above certain thresholds must register with the [Oregon Government Ethics Commission](https://www.oregon.gov/ogec/pages/lobbying-overview.aspx) and file quarterly spending reports that anyone can [search](https://www.oregon.gov/ogec/public-records/Pages/Public-Records-Search.aspx). {added: OGEC. Verify whether any lobbying rules apply to local governments.}
- EDCO’s Jon Stark also said AWS will no longer require communities to sign nondisclosure agreements. {gap: link to source requested from Jon Stark}

**On the other hand, there could also be good reasons for these private dealings to remain private.** Economic Development for Central Oregon (EDCO) says early recruitment is often kept confidential to protect companies’ competitive information and workers. It calls these confidentiality agreements a real limit, not bad faith, though it often means the public learns of a project after major terms are set. EDCO believes the appropriate places for public input are elected officials, its three-year community strategy meetings, and public votes on long-term incentives. {src: EDCO written submission, Oct 13; CC Mtg 4 Pt 1, Oct 14}
