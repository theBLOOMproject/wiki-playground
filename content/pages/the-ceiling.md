**The “ceiling” is how much power the public could have, beyond what the law requires.** One well-known way to picture this is Sherry Arnstein’s “Ladder of Citizen Participation” (1969). Each rung up gives residents more real say over the outcome. {added: Arnstein, S. (1969), “A Ladder of Citizen Participation,” Journal of the American Institute of Planners 35(4)}

::: figure

Arnstein’s ladder is a critique, and it treats higher rungs as better. A newer, more neutral tool is the IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation (Inform, Consult, Involve, Collaborate, Empower), which treats each level as fitting some decisions but not others. Whether every decision should reach the top is a question for delegates. {added: IAP2 Spectrum; add link}

**Models raised by the team and Committee:**

- **Participatory budgeting.** Residents decide directly how to spend part of a public budget. *(Participatory Budgeting Oregon)*
- **Electrify Ashland**, a Rogue Climate campaign in Ashland, Oregon. {verify: details}
- **Community benefit agreements.** A developer signs an agreement with community groups promising specific local benefits in return for their support.
- **Michigan’s Affordability and Responsible Growth Pledge** (July 16, 2026), signed by six companies. {verify: details}
- **Civic assemblies** (see [Deliberation](#during-a-decision)).
