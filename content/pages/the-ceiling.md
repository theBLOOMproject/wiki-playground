::: keypoints
- The “ceiling” is how much power the public could have, beyond what the law requires.
- Having a chance to engage isn’t the same as being able to use it. Schedules, language, distance, internet access, and trust all get in the way.
- Arnstein’s ladder (1969) runs from manipulation at the bottom to citizen control at the top, and treats higher rungs as better.
- The newer IAP2 Spectrum treats each level as fitting some decisions but not others.
:::

**The “ceiling” is how much power the public could have, beyond what the law requires.** Depending on funding and interest, public officials can create more opportunities for meaningful public engagement.

{team: Add from NCL: limits of common engagement methods and what deliberation adds. Candidate: Nabatchi and Leighninger, Public Participation for 21st Century Democracy (2015).}

**Who gets to take part?** Having a chance to engage is not the same as being able to use it. Meetings may happen during working hours, go on for hours, or follow rules that limit what people can say. {src: Utah book} Barriers in Central Oregon include language and interpretation, disability access, work and caregiving schedules, distance and transportation, internet access, and trust in institutions. These look different across ages: a working parent, a retiree, and a high school student each face different hurdles. Two questions to keep in mind for every stage: who is missing, and who keeps getting asked? {src: Taha Elwefati, CC Mtg 1, Sep 25; Andrea Wasilew, CC Mtg 3, Oct 9; Kathi Barguil and Rachel Haakenson, CC Mtg 4 Pt 1, Oct 14; Karen Rush, Oct 15; USDOT, Promising Practices (2022)}

**Increasing citizen power.** One well-known way to picture this is Sherry Arnstein’s “Ladder of Citizen Participation” (1969). Each rung up gives residents more real say over the outcome. {added: Arnstein, S. (1969), “A Ladder of Citizen Participation,” Journal of the American Institute of Planners 35(4)}

Arnstein’s ladder is a critique, and it treats higher rungs as better. A newer, more neutral tool is the IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation (Inform, Consult, Involve, Collaborate, Empower), which treats each level as fitting some decisions but not others. Whether every decision should reach the top is a question for delegates. {added: IAP2 Spectrum; add link}
