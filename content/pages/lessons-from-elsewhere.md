::: keypoints
- AI decisions are complex, opaque, and high stakes, but other places have faced problems with similar traits.
- Three short case studies show what people put in place, what worked, and what didn’t.
:::

As discussed in the previous section, decisions related to AI in the public sector face a number of unique challenges: the underlying technology is complex and often opaque, and the impacts are likely huge but difficult to pin down because the phenomenon is so new. Even still, many cities and counties across the country have dealt with similar challenges and found ways to involve the public in meaningful ways. These examples are not all related to artificial intelligence, but reflect many of the same characteristics (complex, uncertain, high stakes) in one way or another.

Each short case study covers:

- Some basic information about the circumstances
- What the people implemented
- What worked well, and what didn’t

::: cards
- [Loudoun County shifts away from by-right approval of data centers](#case-loudoun) — After two decades of courting the industry, the Virginia county with the world’s largest concentration of data centers changed its zoning rules so that every new data center must go through public hearings and a vote by elected officials, rather than a staff-level sign-off.
- [The Hanford Advisory Board on nuclear waste](#case-hanford) — After four decades of plutonium production tainted the Columbia River watershed, a 37-person board representing different sectors of Washington and Oregon was assembled to present consensus-based recommendations to federal departments on how to clean it up.
- [Oakland’s Privacy Commission on surveillance technologies](#case-oakland) — When residents learned the city planned to turn a port security system into a citywide surveillance hub, a coalition of residents, community groups, and privacy advocates pushed the city to scale it back, and won a standing citizen commission that reviews every surveillance technology the city wants to use before the City Council decides.
:::
