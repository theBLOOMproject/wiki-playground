*Goes with [the ceiling](#when-to-get-involved).*

**Increasing citizen power.** One well-known way to picture this is Sherry Arnstein’s “Ladder of Citizen Participation” (1969). Each rung up gives residents more real say over the outcome.

::: figure
{{> ladder}}

Arnstein’s ladder of citizen participation (1969), from the lowest rung at the bottom to the highest at the top.
:::

Arnstein’s ladder is a critique, and it treats higher rungs as better. A newer, more neutral tool is the IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation (Inform, Consult, Involve, Collaborate, Empower), which treats each level as fitting some decisions but not others. Whether every decision should reach the top is a question for delegates.
